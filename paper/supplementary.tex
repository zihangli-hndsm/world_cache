This supplement provides the complete per-pair audit and the source-only
empirical gate details for the 17-pair annotated subset. All candidate selection
and spatial consensus decisions are made without the hidden 3RScan alignment;
matching uses every depth-valid sampled point, rescan observations are stored
in native scan coordinates, and annotation labels are used only for post-hoc
scoring and pose evaluation.

\section{Relation inclusion, target choice, and scan overlap}

The headline set is an availability-selected subset of the 1,004 official
reference/rescan relations listed in 3RScan metadata, not a random sample from
that universe. Inclusion required complete RGB-D sequence archives for both
scans and the corresponding annotation meshes to be available to the evaluator.
The original matched 14-relation analysis comprised 13 source pairs and the
pre-designated held-out target. Three additional source relations (pair06--08)
were added without changing the target or the evaluation protocol. The target
was therefore preserved from the initial split rather than randomly sampled.
The table gives all 17 relation identities; each side contributed the first
20 frames in manifest order. No reference or rescan UUID is reused across the
17 relations (34 distinct scan UUIDs). This scan-level disjointness does not
show that capture locations are physically independent: cross-relation site
overlap was not verified. Pair-bootstrap intervals below describe variation
across relation units and should not be read as confirmed independent-site
uncertainty.

\section{Candidate-set availability audit}

The evaluator optionally records whether any of the top-20 descriptor
candidates has the query's semantic or instance label. These fields are
computed only after candidate construction and consensus, using annotations
for audit purposes; they do not affect descriptor ranking, RANSAC, or the
reported clean metrics. A correct semantic candidate is available for 79.7\%
of source queries and 96.6\% of target queries. Only 25.8\% and 63.6\%,
respectively, become correct semantic inliers; conditional recovery given a
candidate hit is 32.4\% on source and 65.8\% on target.

The pair-level contrast is more informative than the aggregate. For 4aca,
semantic candidate recall is 19.5\% and correct-inlier coverage is 0.0\%;
this is a genuine candidate-set omission. For 95be, semantic candidate recall
is 69.2\%, but only 1.0\% of candidate-hit queries become correct inliers,
indicating wrong-consensus/pose selection rather than simple absence of the
label. The audit also records 208 geometric inliers for 4aca whose candidate
sets contain no correct semantic label, making explicit that a spatially
coherent hypothesis is not automatically semantically valid.

\section{Top-K sensitivity}

We rerun the complete clean protocol over $K\in\{1,5,10,20,50\}$, keeping
the ViT-G blocks $(2,20,40)$, the $0.10$~m consensus threshold, the RANSAC
budget, and the source/target split fixed. The sweep is an audit of the fixed
$K=20$ operating point; no target result is used to choose $K$. On the source
pairs, $K=1$ has the highest weighted retained semantic accuracy but only 11.2\%
coverage. Among settings retaining at least 20\% weighted source coverage,
$K=10$ has the highest retained semantic accuracy (89.3\%), while $K=20$ gives the larger
coverage-oriented operating point used in the main paper (30.4\%). The
held-out target follows the same qualitative pattern up to $K=20$; widening

\section{RANSAC seed sensitivity}

The primary tables fix RANSAC seed 17 for exact reproducibility. To measure
stochastic sensitivity without changing descriptors, candidates, threshold, or
iteration budget, we rerun every pair with seeds 17, 29, and 41. The held-out
target is stable: retained semantic accuracy ranges from 93.1\% to 93.5\% and
coverage from 67.6\% to 68.2\%. The source aggregate is more sensitive,
ranging from 83.1\% to 88.0\% retained semantic accuracy at 30.3--30.8\% coverage;
the variation is concentrated in wrong-pose pairs 95be, pair04, and pair06.
Thus spatial consensus is a selective verifier, not a deterministic global-pose

\section{Descriptor-permutation negative control}

To test whether spatial consensus alone could explain the semantic result, we
repeat the exact ViT-G protocol after randomly permuting the reference
descriptor rows while leaving reference points, labels, query points, $K=20$,
the $0.10$~m threshold, and the 1,500-iteration budget unchanged. The
permutation is fixed with seed 17 and is applied before candidate construction;
it is not used to tune any threshold. Table~\ref{tab:negative-control} shows
that the source aggregate falls to 46.3\% retained semantic accuracy at 10.5\%
coverage, with no strict pose success. On the held-out target, coverage and
retained semantic accuracy fall to 7.9\% and 3.6\%, respectively, with a
$162.2^\circ$ rotation error. This negative control supports the claim that
the normal result depends on descriptor--point identity rather than on the
RANSAC verifier alone; the residual semantic floor on source pairs reflects
category-level coincidences, not successful correspondence.
At the paired scene level, normal matching is better on 15/16 pairs for both
coverage and retained semantic accuracy; the macro semantic gap is 38.9 percentage
points (76.3\% versus 37.4\%).

\section{Frame-level selection}

\section{Source-only empirical gate fitting}

The residual selector is fit on all 16 source pairs at a requested 90\%
retained semantic-accuracy target, then applied once to the target. We call
this an empirical source-fit gate rather than a calibration guarantee.
Table~\ref{tab:calibration} also reports representative leave-one-pair-out
(LOO) fits. LOO thresholds are
fit without the listed pair, and the reported coverage and retained semantic
accuracy are on the held-out pair.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\scriptsize
\resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{\input{generated_calibration_table.tex}}
\caption{Empirical source-fit residual gate. The all-source row is evaluated on the target;
LOO rows are evaluated on the held-out source pair.}
\label{tab:calibration}
\end{table}

The all-source fit retains 236 of 1,069 target queries (22.1\%) at 96.6\%
retained semantic accuracy. LOO gate fitting exposes the limitation of residual-only
selection: held-out 4aca and 95be reach only 0.0\% and 10.0\% retained
semantic accuracy, respectively. The candidate audit above shows that 4aca is
candidate-set limited, while 95be has substantial candidate availability but
fails consensus recovery; neither is fixed by a threshold choice. Cross-fitted
LOO pooling retains 1,364/15,048 source queries (9.1\%) at 76.6\% retained
semantic accuracy; only 13/16 folds have a 95\% threshold, pooling 115 queries
at 85.2\% accuracy. This is a transfer audit, not a universal guarantee. The
threshold search scans residual prefixes and uses a one-sided empirical target;
it does not provide a family-wise finite-sample guarantee.

The 0.03304~m source-fit threshold retains 6.1\% of source queries with 91.7\%
observed retained semantic accuracy and 14.1\% of target queries at 98.7\% retained accuracy and
97.1\% semantic mIoU (Table~\ref{tab:risk-controlled}). However, the threshold
was selected after scanning 14,949 residual prefixes, so its raw 90.1\%
Clopper--Pearson lower bound is descriptive rather than family-wise valid. A
Bonferroni correction over all prefixes produces no threshold satisfying a 90\%
bound; the maximum adjusted lower bound is 87.4\%. For an auditable fixed
point, a 0.02~m gate gives an unadjusted 90.9\% source lower bound at 1.9\%
source coverage, and 3.3\% target coverage at 100\% retained semantic accuracy. Since
this fixed point is also chosen retrospectively, it is an audit diagnostic,
not a family-wise guarantee. The pair-bootstrap intervals and LOO failures
remain the relevant evidence about scene heterogeneity.

and a 99.85\% non-zero label rate. Only 0.31\% of samples are farther than
0.30~m; these points are retained rather than silently filtered. The complete
per-side audit is included with the released diagnostics.

\section{Reproducibility and scope}

\paragraph{Artifact boundary.}
The anonymized artifact release contains the paper sources, lightweight
diagnostics, code, and consistency verifier; the venue-facing PDF package
contains only the main paper and this supplement. Raw 3RScan frame archives
and model checkpoints remain external inputs and are not silently bundled.

\paragraph{Protocol comparability.}
DUSt3R, MASt3R, and VGGT are adjacent RGB foundation models whose standard
outputs are pointmaps, image-level matches, or multi-view geometry; SemReg and
ML-SemReg are trained semantic-registration systems. We do not report them as
direct numeric baselines because porting those outputs to the saved RGB-D
frame-bank, native-coordinate candidate pool, and post-hoc semantic
label-transfer denominator would define a different experiment. FPFH and FCGF
are therefore the controlled geometry-only baselines under the exact protocol
used here, and no state-of-the-art registration claim is made. No MASt3R smoke
result is included in this dated snapshot because a one-frame image-match
experiment would not define the paper's frame-bank or semantic-transfer
denominator.
